Key findings
Finding 1 : Career Pathways

No single path. Many different routes.

Design leaders come from different backgrounds. Our research represents 93 distinct pathways, across different discipline backgrounds, education levels, organization types, and career levels.

Finding 2 : Starting Points

Starting points define the shape of the design career

Where you start shapes where you go. Leaders who began as freelancers or independents are 9× more likely to found a company. Those who started inside large design organizations are significantly more likely to end up on the executive track.

Finding 3 : Tool Stack

The industry is changing. So are its tools.

When asked how much their tool stack would change over the next five years, only 7% of respondents expected moderate or little change — a signal of deeper shifts in process and practice.

About this project

This site summarizes quantitative findings from a 2026 survey conducted by Design Leadership, a seminar led by Doug Powell for Washington University in St. Louis. This seminar brings together undergraduate and graduate students from across disciplines to examine how design shapes and is shaped by the organizations it lives inside.

The survey results represent 119 design leaders: how they entered the field, their first organizations, current level, tool expectations, and outlook on the profession. Three main findings are presented as chapters; the explore view shows all 30 questions with optional filters for age, gender, and region.

Survey of 119 design leaders (54 female, 63 male, 2 non-binary) · 71% North America, 29% global · Fielded 2026
Statistical methods: Mann-Whitney U, Fisher's exact, Chi-square, Spearman correlations, logistic regression · Significance threshold: p < 0.05
